If you've tried to submit to arXiv for the first time, you may have been told you need an "endorsement". This article explains what that means, why arXiv uses it, and what changed in January 2026.

ResearchGuild is an independent, open-source community. We are **not affiliated with arXiv**, and we can't endorse you or change arXiv's decisions. The linked arXiv pages are the final word.

## What endorsement is

arXiv's endorsement page says arXiv requires users to be **endorsed before submitting their first paper to arXiv or to a new category**. An endorsement is a statement, made through arXiv's own form, by an established arXiv author that you are someone who can reasonably contribute to a given subject area.

A few things endorsement is **not**:

- **It is not peer review.** arXiv says so explicitly. An endorser is not expected to verify that your results are correct.
- **It is not a guarantee of acceptance.** Endorsed submissions still go through moderation.
- **It is not a one-time pass for everything.** Endorsement is tied to **endorsement domains**, groups of related subject areas. Being endorsed in one domain doesn't automatically cover another.

## Why arXiv uses endorsement

arXiv describes endorsement as a way to verify that contributors belong to the scientific community in a way that is fair and can scale with arXiv's growth, while keeping costs far lower than formal peer review.

## The January 2026 change

This is the most important recent update for new authors.

Previously, having an email address at a recognised academic institution could be enough for automatic endorsement. arXiv first changed this for its mathematics section in December 2025, and then, **as of 21 January 2026, per arXiv**, extended it to all sections: arXiv **no longer accepts institutional email addresses as the sole qualifier of endorsement for new authors**.

arXiv said the change was made to support its community of authors, readers, volunteer moderators and staff by stemming a flood of low-quality, non-scientific submissions.

### The two paths now

According to arXiv's January 2026 announcement, new submitters are endorsed in one of two ways:

1. **Automatic endorsement.** This requires **both** an institutional email address from an academic or research institution **and** previous authorship on an existing paper accepted to the arXiv endorsement domain you want to submit to.
2. **Personal endorsement.** If you can't meet both requirements, you seek personal endorsement directly from an established arXiv author in the same endorsement domain.

For most students and first-time researchers, **path 2 is the realistic one**. Even with a university email address, if you've never been an author on an arXiv paper in that domain, you'll probably need someone to endorse you personally.

## How do I know if I need one?

You don't need to guess. arXiv's submission overview explains that when you start a new submission and select your category, if endorsement is required you'll be notified and receive an endorsement request email with instructions. See [Getting your endorsement code](/learn/getting-your-endorsement-code).

## How many endorsements, and can I lose one?

- arXiv says **at least one positive endorsement** is required per endorsement domain.
- arXiv's endorsement page also says endorsers can record that they **do not** wish to endorse someone, and that positive endorsements need to outnumber negative ones. We explain the endorser's side in [How to decline kindly](/learn/how-to-decline-kindly).
- arXiv reserves the right to revoke endorsement if a submitter violates arXiv's policies or code of conduct.

## What endorsers are asked to check

arXiv asks endorsers to either know you or see the paper you intend to submit, and to check that it is appropriate for the subject area. They are not expected to read it in detail or verify correctness. arXiv says endorsers should not endorse if the author is unfamiliar with the basic facts of the field or if the work is entirely disconnected from current work in the area. See [What an endorser is (and isn't) responsible for](/learn/endorser-responsibilities).
