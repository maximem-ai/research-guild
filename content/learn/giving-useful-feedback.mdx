For a first-time author, a few thoughtful comments from an experienced researcher can be the difference between a confusing rejection and a smooth submission. This article suggests how to make your feedback on ResearchGuild as useful as possible, without turning it into a full journal review.

ResearchGuild is independent and not affiliated with arXiv. The arXiv checks mentioned below are summarised from arXiv's public pages.

## Remember what you're helping with

Pre-submission feedback on ResearchGuild has two goals:

1. Help the author **get through arXiv's process smoothly**: right category, right content type, files and language in order.
2. Help the author **communicate their work clearly** to researchers in the area.

It isn't a verdict on whether the work would be accepted at a top venue. arXiv's moderation checks that submissions are topical and refereeable scholarly contributions; that's a useful bar to keep in mind.

## A practical checklist

### 1. Fit and content type

- Is this a **research paper**, a **survey**, or a **position paper**? In CS, as of October 2025, per arXiv, reviews and position papers need documentation of successful peer review, or they're likely to be rejected. If the paper reads like a survey, tell the author early. See [Research paper vs survey vs position paper](/learn/research-survey-position-papers).
- Does the paper engage with **current work** in the area? arXiv tells endorsers not to endorse work that's entirely disconnected from current work.

### 2. Category

- Does the chosen **primary category** match the likely readership?
- Are **cross-lists** sensible? arXiv says it is rarely appropriate to add more than one or two.

### 3. Title and abstract

- Could a busy moderator tell what the paper contributes from these alone?
- Are claims specific and supported, rather than broad and promotional?

### 4. Structure and substance

- Is the main contribution clearly stated?
- Are methods described well enough that an expert could follow them?
- Are limitations acknowledged?
- Are references relevant and reasonably up to date?

You don't need to verify every result. If something looks clearly wrong or missing, though, it's kind to say so.

### 5. Language and format

- Is there a **full English version**, placed first if there's also another language? arXiv requires this as of February 2026.
- Do figures, tables and references render correctly?
- If significant **generative AI** use seems likely, is it disclosed? arXiv's policy asks authors to report significant use of such tools and holds them responsible for all content.

## How to write feedback that helps

### Lead with the most important point

If the category is wrong or the paper is really a survey, say that first. Minor typos can wait.

### Be specific

Instead of "the related work is weak", try "Section 2 doesn't mention recent work on X, such as [paper]; readers in cs.CL will expect a comparison."

### Separate "must fix" from "nice to have"

A short structure works well:

- **Before submitting to arXiv:** issues likely to affect moderation.
- **To strengthen the paper:** improvements that matter for readers or later peer review.
- **Small things:** typos, formatting.

### Explain the why

First-time authors learn most when they understand the reason: "Moderators may reclassify this to cs.LG because your contribution is a training method, not a language task."

### Be kind and direct

Assume good faith. Many authors are students, working in a second language, or without an advisor. Direct feedback delivered kindly is a gift.

## What to avoid

- Rewriting the paper for them.
- Asking for major new experiments unless the paper can't stand without them.
- Comments on the author's background, institution or English beyond what affects clarity.
- Sharing, quoting or reusing the manuscript. arXiv asks endorsers to treat papers as confidential, and so do we. See [Manuscript confidentiality](/learn/manuscript-confidentiality).

## After feedback: endorse, or not

Once you've given feedback, you may decide whether to endorse. That decision follows arXiv's criteria (see [What an endorser is (and isn't) responsible for](/learn/endorser-responsibilities)) and is recorded on arXiv's form. If you decide not to, read [How to decline kindly](/learn/how-to-decline-kindly).
