When you submit to arXiv you'll be asked to pick a license. It's easy to click through, but this choice decides how other people may reuse your work, and you can't take it back. This guide explains the options in plain language.

This is general information from arXiv's public pages, not legal advice. ResearchGuild is independent and not affiliated with arXiv. For anything that depends on your situation, read the [arXiv Licenses page](https://info.arxiv.org/help/license/index.html) and talk to your institution or publisher.

## Two things to know first

1. **The license is irrevocable.** arXiv says articles are a permanent part of the scholarly record and the license chosen for each version is irrevocable. arXiv also notes that different versions of the same work can carry different licenses.
2. **You must have the right to grant it.** arXiv says the submitter chooses the license and must certify that they have the right to grant it. If a publisher already holds copyright, or your employer owns the work, check with them first.

arXiv also says it **will not remove an announced article to comply with journal policy**. That makes checking your target journal's preprint and licensing policy before you submit especially important. arXiv suggests checking funder and journal policies directly or through a service such as Sherpa Romeo.

## The options arXiv offers

The descriptions below paraphrase arXiv's Licenses page.

| License | Can others reuse it? | Commercial use? | Adaptations? |
|---|---|---|---|
| CC BY 4.0 | Yes, with credit | Allowed | Allowed |
| CC BY-SA 4.0 | Yes, with credit | Allowed | Allowed, but must use the same license |
| CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 | Yes, with credit | Non-commercial only | Allowed, but must use the same license |
| CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 | Yes, with credit | Non-commercial only | Not allowed (unadapted copies only) |
| CC0 | Yes, no conditions | Allowed | Allowed |
| arXiv perpetual, non-exclusive license | Limited | Limits reuse by others | Limits reuse by others |

### CC BY 4.0

Anyone may distribute, remix, adapt and build on your work in any medium, including commercially, as long as they credit you. This is a common choice for open access.

### CC BY-SA 4.0

Like CC BY, but anyone who adapts your work must license their adaptation under identical terms ("share-alike").

### CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and CC BY-NC-ND 4.0

Both restrict reuse to non-commercial purposes and require credit. NC-SA allows adaptations under the same license; NC-ND allows only unadapted copies.

### CC0

A public-domain dedication. You give up your copyright, and anyone may reuse the work for any purpose with no conditions. arXiv notes this is the one option under which you don't keep ownership.

### arXiv perpetual, non-exclusive license

This gives arXiv limited rights to distribute your article, and limits reuse of any type by other people or organisations. It is the most restrictive option for reusers.

## Who keeps the copyright?

Per arXiv, for all these licenses **except CC0**, the original copyright holder keeps ownership after posting. Choosing CC BY does not mean giving your copyright to arXiv; it means granting everyone certain permissions in advance.

## How to choose

Ask yourself:

- **Does my funder or institution require a particular license?** Many open-access mandates specify one.
- **What does my target journal allow?** Some journals accept preprints under any license; others have preferences. Check before submitting.
- **Do I want others to be able to reuse figures, text or data in teaching materials, datasets or commercial products?** If yes, CC BY or CC0. If you want to prevent commercial reuse, an NC option.
- **Are my co-authors in agreement?** The license applies to the whole paper.

If you aren't sure, it's better to pause and ask your advisor or library than to pick quickly.

## Licenses and new versions

When you replace a paper, you create a new version. arXiv says each version's license is irrevocable, and that versions may have different licenses. Read the Licenses page before replacing, and see [Replacing, versioning and withdrawing](/learn/replacing-versioning-withdrawing).
