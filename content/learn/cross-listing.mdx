Once you've chosen a primary category, arXiv lets you add secondary categories, known as **cross-lists**. A cross-list makes your paper appear in another category's listing too, so that a second community of readers can find it. Used well, cross-listing helps interdisciplinary work reach the right people. Used carelessly, it annoys readers and can be undone by moderators.

Endorse Commons is independent and not affiliated with arXiv. The [arXiv cross-listing page](https://info.arxiv.org/help/cross.html) is the authoritative source.

## What a cross-list does

According to arXiv, cross-listing to another category makes your article appear in the regular listing for that category, in the cross-list section. Your primary category doesn't change; it is still where the paper "lives". Cross-lists, like new submissions and replacements, are made public as part of arXiv's scheduled announcement process.

## When a cross-list is a good idea

A cross-list is appropriate when a meaningful group of researchers in another category would genuinely want to read your paper. Some examples:

- An NLP paper (primary cs.CL) that introduces a training technique of broad interest to machine learning researchers might cross-list to cs.LG.
- A medical-imaging application paper whose primary is the application area might cross-list to cs.CV, which is what arXiv's machine learning classification guide suggests for that kind of paper.
- A statistics-focused ML paper with primary stat.ML is automatically cross-listed to cs.LG, per the same guide, so you don't need to add it yourself.

## When it isn't

arXiv's guidance is direct:

- It is **rarely appropriate** to add more than one or two cross-lists.
- You are unlikely to know that a cross-list is appropriate **unless you read that category yourself**.
- Readers consider **excessive or inappropriate cross-listing to be bad etiquette**, so think about your cross-lists beforehand and don't cross-list without good reason.

A helpful test: have you read several recent papers in that category's listing, and would your paper look at home among them? If you've never browsed the category, that's a sign to leave it off.

Common temptations to resist:

- Adding cs.AI to every AI-flavoured paper "for visibility".
- Cross-listing to every field your application touches.
- Choosing categories based on which are busiest.

## How moderators handle cross-lists

arXiv's moderation page says moderators may add cross-lists to related categories, or remove them when the classification is deemed inappropriate. Classification questions are also one of the reasons a submission can be placed on hold. If a moderator removes a cross-list, that is a normal part of the process; see [After you submit](/learn/after-you-submit) for what happens and how appeals work.

arXiv also asks authors not to contact moderators directly about their submissions. Questions go through arXiv's user support channels.

## How to add a cross-list

- **At submission time:** you choose your primary category and can add cross-lists during the submission process.
- **After announcement:** arXiv's cross-listing page describes a cross-list facility on your user page for adding cross-lists to an existing article. The details of this interface can change, so follow the current instructions on that page.

## Cross-lists and endorsement

Endorsement is tied to where you submit. arXiv's endorsement page says you need endorsement before submitting your first paper or submitting to a new category. We have not found an official statement on whether cross-listing into a new domain requires separate endorsement; if this affects you, check [arXiv's endorsement page](https://info.arxiv.org/help/endorsement.html) or ask arXiv support.

## A simple rule of thumb

Pick one primary. Add zero, one or at most two cross-lists that you, as a reader, would actually expect to see your paper in. If you're unsure, ask a reviewer on Endorse Commons who reads those listings; see [Choosing your primary category](/learn/choosing-primary-category) for how to reason about audiences.
